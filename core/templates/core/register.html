Joining the Team

What opportunities are provided in HFR?

You can choose to work on projects spanning our three divisions: LAND, SEA or AIR.

Each division is composed of sub teams spanning the range of material taught in university, and more.

Land

The largest and oldest team in HFR. Joining this team puts you on the car we race at the Shell Eco-Marathon: chassis, powertrain, aero and telemetry.

Explore the Land Division

Sea

The team that raced Vital Spark at Monaco, now building HFR's own Energy Class boat for the Monaco Energy Boat Challenge.

Explore the Sea Division

Air

Design and build the next generation of aviation transport: our hydrogen-powered aero concept, alongside the UofG Aeronautics Society.

Explore the Air Division

What is the team like?

Our engineering community recruits over 100 engineers from all backgrounds. We take pride in our society's diversity.

What about volunteering?

HFR has been working with STEM Ambassadors ever since its inception. We provide members with the opportunity to volunteer and give back to the next generation.

How could this help my career?

HFR members graduate with real engineering experience, a strong network, and projects you'll be proud to talk about in interviews.

  • CAD & design tools from zero
  • Manufacturing and workshop skills
  • Hydrogen fuel cell fundamentals
  • Electronics, data & telemetry
  • Project & sponsorship management
  • Media, brand and communications

Why join HFR?

We provide an experience unlike any other in UofG. We give members the opportunity to learn and gain experience in a fun and engaging way.

Who do we look for?

Most of our members joined with zero engineering projects behind them. We don't expect experience. We expect curiosity, reliability and enthusiasm.

Why this society? You could build a Formula 1 car, a rocket, a satellite or prosthetics. We want to know why hydrogen racing.

When you apply, you'll answer four questions:

01Why this society?
02Why this division?
03What makes you you?
04What do you want to learn?

A bad answer looks like

"I want to join because it will look good on my CV."

A good answer looks like

"I've never built anything before, but I've always wanted to know how a fuel cell actually works, and I'll show up every week to find out."
